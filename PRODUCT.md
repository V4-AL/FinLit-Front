# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Young adults and students building their first real money habits — budgeting, saving, credit, and investing basics for the first time. They open finlit in short sessions squeezed between other tasks (a commute, a study break, before bed), so the experience needs to reward a few minutes of attention rather than demand sustained focus.

## Product Purpose

finlit teaches financial literacy through bite-sized, gamified daily lessons. It exists to turn "I should learn about money" into an actual daily habit, using streaks, XP, and levels to make the returning behavior feel rewarding. Success is a user who keeps coming back day after day, steadily advancing through modules, and picking up real financial habits along the way — not just a user who completes a single session.

## Positioning

finlit makes financial literacy feel like a game you actually want to play daily. The differentiation is habit-forming, bite-sized gamification — not depth of content, not advisory features.

## Brand Personality

Encouraging, playful, and trustworthy. The tone should feel closer to a habit-building game (think Duolingo's warmth and momentum) than a financial institution, but it can never tip into feeling flimsy or untrustworthy — this is still an app about money, and credibility has to survive the playfulness.

## Anti-references

Should not look or feel like a stuffy corporate bank app: no navy-and-gold palettes, no dense data tables, none of the cold, institutional distance typical of traditional banking software.

## Design Principles

- **Habit over density.** Every screen should optimize for "come back tomorrow," not for cramming in more information. If a design choice trades daily-return motivation for content depth, favor the habit.
- **Playful, never flimsy.** Warmth and game mechanics (streaks, XP, levels) are the voice, but they must sit on layouts and type that still read as credible for a product that touches someone's money.
- **Progress must always be visible.** Streaks, XP, level, and completion state should never be buried — they're the emotional payoff that drives the next session.
- **Momentum first.** Minimize friction between "opened the app" and "started the next lesson." Every extra tap or decision point is a chance to lose the habit loop.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the baseline: body text and placeholders at ≥4.5:1 contrast, large/bold text ≥3:1. Respect the OS-level reduced-motion setting with instant/crossfade alternatives for all animation. No additional accommodations beyond this baseline requested at this time.
